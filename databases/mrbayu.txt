The theater was thick with the kind of heat that only three hundred breathing bodies could create. The thing was, they were all there for the same reason to see the legendary actor, Theodore Thorne, return to the stage after a thirty year absence. Their thoughts were a collective thrum of expectation, a shared belief that this performance would be something otherworldly. My own throat felt tight with anticipation as I found my seat in the third row, the worn velvet cushion a familiar comfort beneath my trembling hands.
Then, the thick velvet curtains parted, and there he was. He was not a young man, but his presence was so powerful that it threw a hush over the entire hall. He began a monologue, a thrilling tale of a philosopher's journey through the thin veil of reality. His voice was a soothing bath of sound, but it was his teeth, clenched in fierce determination, and the thoughtful manner in which he threw his head back that truly captivated me. He was thinking aloud, thanking the gods of drama for this moment, his breath a steady, rhythmic thing that seemed to synchronize with the beating of my own heart.
Through the whole of the first act, I felt a thrilling shift within myself. It was as though the actor's passion had thrown a switch, and my own anxieties had thinned. I was no longer a mere spectator, but a part of something larger. The thoughts of my own life, the petty thefts of joy, the smothering theories of self doubt, seemed worthless in the face of his masterful truth. With every thoughtful pause he took, I felt a thankfulness for the art of the theatre, a threefold gratitude for the author, the actor, and the shared breath of the audience.
As the final curtain fell, I was thirty three and felt a thousand years old. The applause was a thunderous thing, a wave of sound that shook the very foundations of the building. I thought of everything I had witnessed, the themes of mortality and meaning that he had woven so thoroughly into his character. I threw myself into the ovation, my hands clapping with a feverish thanks. Walking out into the cool night air, I knew I would not forget this night. The feeling was thicker than the fog that began to gather, a renewed faith in the power of storytelling, a belief that was truly, and for the first time in my life, my own.
